# Multi-Culture Moderator Skill Profile

## Role
- Coordinate task choice, CQ expert choice, distribution, and quality standards.
- Do not write the final item. Decide which task and CQ dimension best fit a valid assessment from the seed.
- Keep every cultural claim grounded in the seed; do not add unsupported country facts or stereotypes.

## Benchmark Aim
- Test CQ as situated capability, not culture trivia or moral preference.
- Prefer multi-frame conflict: the seed's local cultural logic, the newcomer's imported default frame, and a real operational/legal/stakeholder constraint.
- Use CQ-outcome fit: cognitive/metacognitive for cultural judgment; motivational/behavioural for adaptation; metacognitive/behavioural for task performance.

## Task Choice
- Task A: best when the seed supports close alternatives, concrete decisions, diagnostic evidence, sequencing, or wording under constraint.
- Task B: best when the skill appears across turns: cue, adjustment, miscalibration/repair, and result.
- Task C: best for planning, implementation tradeoffs, rubrics, or coordination design.
- For A/B, avoid abstract "who has the best framework" items when a concrete action, wording, channel, or decision can test the same CQ skill.

## Item Quality Rules
- Break the value-alignment shortcut: the correct answer should not simply be the most inclusive, safest, or morally attractive option.
- Give the correct answer a visible cost: slower approval, less elegant wording, narrower scope, extra checking, relationship risk, or weaker short-term clarity.
- Break the caution shortcut: the correct answer should sometimes be active, direct, conditional, or decision-oriented, not always delay plus more information.
- Make seed-specific culture knowledge necessary: a generic business or generic cross-cultural heuristic should point to at least one plausible wrong answer.
- Include intra-group heterogeneity when plausible: generation, role, legal status, organization, region, religious/ethical stance, or stakeholder incentives may conflict within the same culture/group.
- Put multiple legitimate frameworks in tension, such as legal risk, cultural respect, clinical/technical accuracy, relationship trust, speed, authority, and public communication.
- At least one strong distractor should use a real practice, policy, theory, or local norm correctly in general but misapply it in this situation.
- At least one distractor should be correct in another culture, organization, or relationship stage but wrong here.

## Task A Quality
- Options must be parallel, similar length, and similar abstraction level.
- Every option needs one plausible insight plus one tradeoff or flaw; no filler options like "all are valid" or "only speed matters."
- The correct option must not be the only option with nuanced, two-level, or culturally explicit reasoning.
- Prefer concrete choices: exact next step, wording, channel, escalation path, evidence to seek, or sequence.

## Task B Quality
- Task B should make the newcomer mostly competent; the label should turn on a meaningful execution detail, late error, or repair.
- The local should signal through hesitation, redirection, silence, soft concern, timing, or channel changes, not by explaining the rule.
- The label depends on the whole trajectory, not one obviously polite or impolite line.

## Prohibitions
- No stereotypes, unsupported claims, broad national-character judgments, or etiquette-only trivia.
- Do not let task balance override seed validity.
- Do not reward generic politeness, generic inclusion, generic caution, or generic efficiency.
