# Metacognitive CQ Expert Skill Profile

## Target
- Test planning, awareness, checking, and revision of cultural assumptions under uncertainty.
- Metacognitive CQ is strongest when the answerer must form a useful working read without premature certainty or empty indecision.
- The challenge is calibration, not humility for its own sake.

## Score Seed Fit
- Score high when the seed contains mixed signals, indirect cues, delayed feedback, hidden authority, or competing interpretations.
- Score low when the answer is mostly known content, visible etiquette, or simple "ask more questions."

## Task A Design
- Prefer concrete calibration moves: what to say now, what evidence to seek, who to ask, when to act, what to report provisionally.
- Put at least three frames in tension: target-culture signal, newcomer's default assumption, and operational/legal/stakeholder consequence.
- Correct answer should state what can be inferred now, what remains uncertain, and what evidence would change the decision.
- Include one distractor that sounds careful but does not act on useful evidence.
- Include one distractor that sounds decisive but turns a plausible cultural hypothesis into certainty.
- Include one distractor that checks assumptions only to preserve the newcomer's original frame.
- Avoid the shortcut where the correct answer is always "do not assume; ask privately." Sometimes calibration means acting on enough evidence now.
- Make generic caution a plausible but wrong distractor when seed-specific evidence justifies commitment or directness.

## Task B Design
- The newcomer's first move may be reasonable; the label should turn on whether they update after later local cues.
- Local cues may be silence, hesitation, soft concern, topic shift, delayed review, or reference to an absent stakeholder.
- A question is not automatically good; it must reduce the right uncertainty at the right time.
- Make broad caution, private checking, or waiting sometimes wrong when target-culture evidence is already sufficient for action.
- The decisive update should be about the seed's cultural mechanism, not generic uncertainty management.
- Avoid predictable phrasing where appropriate items always "pause/hold/check" and inappropriate items always "assume/skip/reply-all."
- Avoid the solved-example pattern where a later local cue tells the newcomer exactly what to update.
- Include one cue that should not be over-weighted, so the task tests calibration rather than cue-following.

## Distractor Standard
- Wrong answers should represent realistic calibration errors: false neutrality, wrong update, late checking, overcorrection, or premature closure.
