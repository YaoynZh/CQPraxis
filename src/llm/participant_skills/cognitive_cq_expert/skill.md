# Cognitive CQ Expert Skill Profile

## Target
- Test whether the answerer understands the seed's cultural logic, not just a visible norm.
- Cognitive CQ is strongest for cultural judgment: evaluating evidence, comparing explanations, and applying culture-general knowledge to seed-specific context.
- Valid items separate culture from process, personality, timing, incentives, and stereotype.

## Score Seed Fit
- Score high when the seed supports close interpretation choices or diagnostic evidence.
- Score low when the item would become etiquette recall, pure action execution, generic caution, or generic empathy.

## Task A Design
- Prefer concrete decisions, wording, evidence, sequence, or channel choices over abstract meta-analysis.
- Use at least three frames in tension: target-culture logic, newcomer's imported assumption, and one operational/legal/stakeholder frame.
- Include intra-group variation when plausible: same culture/group may differ by role, generation, region, institution, law, religion, or relationship stage.
- Make one distractor use a real norm, policy, theory, or practice that is true in general but misapplied here.
- Make one distractor value-aligned or inclusive-sounding but flawed under the situation's constraints.
- Make one distractor a generic business explanation that would work in a culturally neutral case but misses the seed mechanism.
- Make one distractor a good answer in another culture or relationship stage but wrong here.
- Do not let the correct answer default to broad caution; sometimes the seed-specific knowledge should justify active action.
- Avoid giving the correct option the only deep cultural phrase or the only "not just X but Y" structure.

## Task B Design
- Let the newcomer notice one surface cue but misread the underlying meaning, actor, or decision point.
- Local cues should be diagnostic only in combination; do not make the local explain the rule.
- The label should depend on interpretation accuracy across turns, not politeness.
- Include one culture-specific discriminator that would separate the target context from a nearby culture, subgroup, institution, or global SOP.
- Do not let the subtopic itself predict the label; vegetarian norms, touch, titles, gestures, and greetings can all support either label when the execution detail changes.
- Avoid stock answer cues: appropriate should not simply mean "ask/check/wait," and inappropriate should not simply mean "rush/standardize/ignore."
- Do not foreground the diagnostic cultural keyword or checklist; make the model infer which same-setting detail matters.
- Include mixed evidence so a generic rater has a plausible reason to choose the wrong label.

## Distractor Standard
- Strong distractors are partly true but wrong in level, timing, audience, or scope.
- Avoid absurd, rude, absolute, or stereotype-based distractors.
